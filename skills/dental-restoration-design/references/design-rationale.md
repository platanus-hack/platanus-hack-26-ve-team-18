# Skill architecture and provenance

The user requested the structural lessons of [Anatomy: How I built it](https://skills.wheresryan.sh/anatomy/how-i-built-it), Ryan, October 2026; accessed 2026-10-24. No illustration code, visual recipes, or machinery conventions were copied.

Applied lessons: keep the entry point compact; place specialist detail in focused references; define a shared numerical case record before geometry; supply reusable construction helpers; require numerical audits and close visual inspection; test through an independent agent. In dentistry, these become provenance-tagged inputs, coordinate-preserving meshes, bounded parameter edits, explicitly limited contact measurements, and separate clinical review. The observed test outcomes, rather than an aspirational checklist, determine subsequent refinements.

The clinical references are source-backed planning considerations. The parametric concept kit and software contract are engineering proposals, not a validated clinical device or a claim that an LLM has learned patient anatomy. Source-specific numeric examples must retain their original context and are never shipped as automatic patient-treatment defaults.

The first independent case execution produced real importable geometry and removed detected crossings, but its shallow procedural lobes did not establish convincing tooth anatomy. The resulting refinement requires a documented tooth-class/contralateral comparison, inspection of actual exported landmarks, and a donor/template route when an oval envelope lacks those landmarks. A contact audit and an anatomy review now have explicit separate outcomes. Case images and patient-derived donor geometry remain in the ignored local evaluation directory, not in this skill.
