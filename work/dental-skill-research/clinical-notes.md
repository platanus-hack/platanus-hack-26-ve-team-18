# Clinical source notes for an implant planning/restoration skill

Research/access date: **2026-10-24 for every URL below**. Scope: clinical considerations and the limits of upper/lower/bite PLY inputs; no patient-specific treatment prescription. Publication dates are distinguished from access dates. Undated documents are identified explicitly.

Evidence labels: **consensus** means professional recommendations informed by reviewed evidence and expert deliberation; **observational** means an association, not proof of causation; **RCT** means randomized clinical evidence within its studied population; **in vitro** means laboratory evidence; **technical** means format or manufacturer documentation. Implementation implications below are identified as **inferences**, rather than validated clinical outputs.

## Claim-to-source mapping for the skill

- **C1 — Separate the objects and treatment stages.** The surgical fixture/body sits in bone; an abutment connects the fixture to the restoration and traverses the mucosa; the crown provides the visible tooth form. A healing abutment, immediate provisional crown, and definitive crown have different purposes. Ask whether this is preoperative fixture planning or restoration of an existing implant before interpreting “implant depth,” “size,” or “contact.” [S1, S2, S12]
- **C2 — Plan from the restoration and reconcile with anatomy.** Establish intended tooth position/form, aesthetics, emergence, restorative space, and connection/retention feasibility, then assess mesiodistal, faciolingual, and apicocoronal fixture position and angulation. Bone availability alone does not establish an appropriate restoration-driven position. Assess medical/dental history, periodontal condition, smoking and other patient/site risks. [S2, S3, S6, S12]
- **C3 — IOS surfaces cannot substitute for internal anatomy.** Current ADA/AAOMR recommendations support CBCT for presurgical implant planning, bone-volume evaluation, and relationships to relevant anatomy. An optical upper/lower/bite mesh does not reveal the bone envelope, roots, mandibular canal, or sinus boundaries. Surface-to-volume registration is recommended in guided workflows. Imaging must follow clinical assessment and justification; do not automatically request repeated CBCT or imply that scan availability replaces interpretation. [S3–S6; PLY limitation is an inference from S18–S19]
- **C4 — “Depth” requires a reference and a system.** Record the selected implant/platform, intended shoulder position relative to the bone crest and planned mucosal/restorative margin, angulation, soft-tissue conditions, and available transmucosal/restorative space. Bone-level and tissue-level designs and their abutments are not interchangeable depth references. There is no universal “place every implant X mm below gingiva” rule supported by these sources. A gum surface alone cannot establish fixture depth. [S1–S2, S9, S12, S20; synthesis/inference]
- **C5 — Diameter and length are dependent choices.** Consider bone height/width and residual walls, neighboring roots/implants, vital anatomy, planned restoration, loading protocol, primary stability, and implant-system indications. Narrower or shorter implants can reduce augmentation or anatomic risk in selected cases, while mechanical and restorative tradeoffs remain. Do not select diameter from crown width alone or automatically maximize length. [S7–S9]
- **C6 — Inspect the entire anatomical relationship and uncertainty.** Relevant structures include adjacent roots/implants, inferior alveolar canal, mental foramen, lingual foramina and mandibular lingual concavities, nasopalatine canal/nasal floor, maxillary sinus, and local pathology. Account for image interpretation, registration, and surgical-transfer error. A virtual mesh clearance is not a surgical clearance guarantee. The clinician must select applicable safety margins. [S3, S6–S7; final sentence is an implementation inference]
- **C7 — Crown emergence must support tissue and cleaning.** Evaluate the abutment–crown transition, mucosal support, facial contour, proximal embrasures, margin/cement accessibility, screw access, and circumferential hygiene/probing access. Avoid overcontouring that obstructs cleaning. Emergence-angle associations are not universal pass/fail thresholds; clinical phenotype, implant design, and restorative context matter. [S2, S9–S11, S20]
- **C8 — Keep proximal and occlusal contacts distinct.** Proximal contacts concern adjacent teeth/restorations and embrasures; occlusal contacts concern the opposing arch. Proximal contact loss and changes in relative occlusal force have been observed over follow-up. A scan cannot establish contact tightness or future stability. Clinical review and maintenance remain necessary. [S13–S14; scan limitation is an inference]
- **C9 — Occlusion is protocol- and patient-dependent.** Establish provisional versus definitive restoration, placement/loading protocol, opposing dentition, parafunction, and the static and excursive relationships being evaluated. ITI recommendations for immediate provisional crowns or short implants must retain their scope. These sources do not justify one numerical occlusal gap, force, cusp angle, or contact-area target for every implant crown. [S7–S8, S12–S14]
- **C10 — Qualify every upper/lower/bite result.** Confirm acquisition/export provenance, common coordinates, units, recorded jaw position, and which files are arches versus buccal bite-registration surfaces. Preserve the imported relationship. Inspect scan coverage and registration before reporting proximity. Independent PLY files may be exported outside occlusion. Static meshes cannot establish patient-specific excursions, condylar paths, contact timing, muscle forces, or clinical overload. User-applied offsets are geometric explorations. [S15–S19; implementation inferences]
- **C11 — Manufacturing requires the actual component workflow.** Record implant manufacturer/family, connection/platform, implant- versus abutment-level acquisition, scanbody identity, matching library/version, selected abutment/Ti-base, material, retention, and laboratory workflow. Use applicable current manufacturer/CAD/CAM instructions for compatible components, screw channels, minimum material thickness, and processing. A generic visual fixture or attractive crown mesh does not establish a manufacturable implant restoration. [S20–S22; implementation inference]

## Primary source notes

### S1 — FDA: component definitions

- **Date:** publication/update date not displayed in retrieved page; accessed 2026-10-24. **Type:** regulator explanation.
- [Dental Implants: What You Should Know](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/dental-devices/dental-implants-what-you-should-know)
- Supports C1: distinguishes the implanted body, abutment, fixation screw, and supported artificial teeth. Useful terminology; not a planning algorithm.

### S2 — ITI: restoration-driven placement and aesthetics

- **Date:** consensus conference 2003; proceedings published 2004. **Type:** consensus, explicitly primarily expert opinion for these aesthetic recommendations.
- [Esthetics in Implant Dentistry](https://academy.iti.org/academy/consensus-database/consensus-statement/-/consensus/esthetics-in-implant-dentistry/1319)
- Supports C1–C4/C7: implant selection follows anatomy and planned restoration; position matters in three dimensions; provisional emergence profiles shape tissue; deep margins complicate seating and cement removal. Scope is especially the aesthetic zone. Do not convert its comfort-zone concept into a universal depth preset.

### S3 — ADA/AAOMR: current imaging recommendations

- **Date:** January 2026, JADA 157(1):20–35; public release 2026-01-05. **Type:** consensus clinical recommendations; authors explicitly distinguish them from formal guidelines because of evidence limitations.
- [Original report, DOI 10.1016/j.adaj.2025.10.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adaj.2025.10.013); [full report hosted by Rhode Island Department of Health](https://health.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur1006/files/2026-01/ADA-AAOMR-patient-selection.pdf)
- Supports C2–C3/C6: Box 1 requires prior clinical assessment and imaging justification; Box 2 §7 recommends CBCT for presurgical planning, anatomy, and bone volume. Restoration delivery and maintenance use indicated 2D radiographs. This newer report should accompany historical 2012 citations.

### S4 — AAOMR: implant-site assessment and anatomy

- **Date:** June 2012, OOOO 113:817–826. **Type:** advisory consensus position statement.
- [Original AAOMR-hosted paper](https://aaomr.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Position%20Papers/aaomr_implants_position_paper.pdf), DOI 10.1016/j.oooo.2012.03.005.
- Supports C3/C6: pp. 821–822 describe ridge dimensions/orientation, sinus and nasal boundaries, foramina/canals, neurovascular structures, and concavities; imaging findings must be matched to the prosthetic plan. Historically recommends cross-sectional assessment of all implant sites and favors CBCT. Read with S3, not as the newest statement.

### S5 — EAO: justification and optimization

- **Date:** first published 2012-03-21, reporting the 2011 consensus workshop. **Type:** consensus imaging guidance.
- [Harris et al., EAO diagnostic-imaging recommendations](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1600-0501.2012.02441.x)
- Supports C3: imaging exposure requires patient benefit, justification, and dose optimization, considering alternatives. Publisher abstract inspected; full recommendation text was not available through that page. Do not infer additional detailed EAO selection rules from this abstract alone.

### S6 — ITI: guided planning and transfer accuracy

- **Date:** 2018 ITI consensus. **Type:** consensus informed by heterogeneous clinical studies.
- [Accuracy of Static Computer-Aided Implant Surgery](https://academy.iti.org/academy/consensus-database/consensus-statement/-/consensus/accuracy-of-static-computer-aided-implant-surgery/1820)
- Supports C2–C3/C6: restoration-driven planning; align surface/prosthetic scans with volumetric/DICOM data; surgical execution has nonzero deviation. The report recommends a **2 mm safety margin for its static guided-surgery context**. Preserve that attribution if cited; do not present it as a universal guarantee, an IOS-derived safety margin, or a replacement for case/system-specific judgment.

### S7 — ITI: implant length

- **Date:** 2018 ITI consensus. **Type:** consensus informed by randomized trials, chiefly posterior sites.
- [Short versus longer implants in posterior jaws](https://academy.iti.org/academy/consensus-database/consensus-statement/-/consensus/survival-rates-of-short-dental-implants-6-mm-compared-with-implants-longer-than-6-mm-in-posterior-jaw-areas/1806)
- Supports C5–C6/C9: length depends on patient and local anatomy; short implants can reduce augmentation morbidity or risk to adjacent structures. The review provided no specific occlusal prescription and advises overload consideration and reassessment. Its size/splinting recommendations retain the studied short-implant context.

### S8 — ITI: implant diameter

- **Date:** 2018 ITI consensus. **Type:** consensus; much of the underlying narrow-diameter evidence had high risk of bias.
- [Narrow-Diameter Implants](https://academy.iti.org/academy/consensus-database/consensus-statement/-/consensus/narrow-diameter-implants/1807)
- Supports C5/C9: narrower implants may preserve spacing and reduce lateral augmentation; reduced diameter increases component/implant fracture concern and requires caution with parafunction or malocclusion. Evidence for survival is not equivalent to proof of equal long-term success for every indication.

### S9 — ITI: bone walls and soft tissue

- **Date:** first published 2023-09-26. **Type:** consensus based on two systematic reviews.
- [Jensen et al., bone dimensions and soft-tissue augmentation](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/clr.14154)
- Supports C4–C5/C7: residual facial bone and soft-tissue conditions influence outcomes. For **healed sites**, the group recommends a buccal wall >1.5 mm at placement; thinner walls showed less favorable outcomes. This is a scoped recommendation, not a PLY-measurable quantity or a universal formula. Selected management can include augmentation or changes to implant diameter/position; those are clinical decisions.

### S10 — EAO: contour and hygiene

- **Date:** first published 2021-10-12. **Type:** consensus drawing on systematic/critical reviews; individual supporting designs vary.
- [Schwarz et al., EAO peri-implantitis consensus](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/clr.13827); [original PDF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/clr.13827)
- Supports C7: avoid obstructive overcontouring and ensure circumferential plaque-removal access. Contour associations relied substantially on cross-sectional studies; evidence about retention type and junction location was inconclusive. Do not assert that any one material, margin position, or retention method prevents disease in all cases.

### S11 — Original clinical studies: emergence profile

- **Dates/types:** Katafuchi et al., online 2017-12-05, issue February 2018, **cross-sectional**; Endres et al., online 2025-08-20, issue November 2025, **RCT**.
- [Katafuchi et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28985447/), DOI 10.1111/jcpe.12829: 83 patients/168 included implants; larger emergence angles and convexity were associated with peri-implantitis in the bone-level subgroup, not the tissue-level subgroup. The **30° split was a study comparison**, not a causal universal limit.
- [Endres et al., three-year RCT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12531365/), DOI 10.1111/jcpe.70018: 47 enrolled/42 re-examined; convex profiles had more recession than concave profiles in studied aesthetic-zone crowns. Small sample and wide confidence interval; recession outcome does not establish a universal disease-prevention contour. Supports C7 with distinct outcomes/designs.

### S12 — ITI: immediate provisional crowns have specific recommendations

- **Date:** 2023 ITI consensus. **Type:** consensus on the Type 1A immediate-placement/restoration/loading protocol in the aesthetic zone.
- [Clinical performance and recommendations](https://academy.iti.org/academy/consensus-database/consensus-statement/-/consensus/clinical-performance-of-immediately-placed-and-immediately-loaded-single-implants-in-the-esthetic-zone/2304)
- Supports C1–C2/C4/C9: assess the site and adjacent teeth, CBCT/periapical imaging, restorative position and abutment options. For its immediate provisional protocol, recommends no eccentric contacts, light proximal contacts, suitable emergence, and manufacturer-specific tightening. Do not generalize these statements to every definitive crown or derive a numerical clearance from them.

### S13 — Original clinical study: proximal contact loss

- **Date:** first published 2022-02-23. **Type:** retrospective observational study.
- [Gasser et al., ten-year follow-up](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/clr.13908)
- Supports C8: 39 patients/80 implants; contact loss was common at long follow-up and mesial contacts were more susceptible. Contact loss was associated with greater probing depth; causation was not established. Its 0.1 mm classification threshold is a measurement convention, not recommended contact tightness or an acceptable design gap.

### S14 — Original clinical study: occlusal change

- **Date:** published 2022-02-07. **Type:** five-year prospective observational study; publisher abstract inspected, full text subscription restricted.
- [Ding et al., posterior single crowns](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00784-022-04394-0)
- Supports C8–C9: relative occlusal force changed in 37 crowns/33 participants. Authors describe its association with marginal bone level as of limited clinical significance. Instrumented relative-force measurements are not mesh distances; this study supplies no geometric safe-load threshold.

### S15 — Original clinical study: static bite registration

- **Date:** published 2022-12-01. **Type:** randomized clinical comparison of registration precision.
- [Morsy and El Kateb](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12903-022-02612-5)
- Supports C10: nine fully dentate participants, one IOS; buccal scans related upper/lower arches in maximum intercuspation. Digital records were more repeatable than tested conventional workflows. **Precision was measured, not trueness**. Do not export its results to every scanner, implant/full-edentulous situation, or dynamic bite.

### S16 — Original laboratory study: additional positions are not continuous movement

- **Date:** published 2024-08-20. **Type:** in vitro, one IOS and articulated casts.
- [Morsy and Hammad, multi-occlusion recording](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11361818/), DOI 10.4047/jap.2024.16.4.221.
- Supports C10: the tested workflow missed lateral-position contacts more often than intercuspal/protrusive contacts. It tested separate recorded positions, not continuous patient movement; the movement-video function was not evaluated. Experimental proximity classes are not clinical occlusion standards.

### S17 — Manufacturer: scan-export orientation varies

- **Date:** publication/update date not displayed; accessed 2026-10-24. **Type:** Align/iTero technical documentation, not clinical validation.
- [Export Settings](https://prod-third-party-partners.iterocloud.com/solutions/dpms/documents/export-settings/)
- Supports C10: documented options include combined arches in bite, separate arches in occlusion, and independently oriented arches; PLY is available. These listed options are in its orthodontic export section. Do not assume all restorative exports share them or that file names establish alignment.

### S18 — Original PLY specification

- **Date:** copyright 1994; finer publication date not stated. **Type:** original technical specification by Greg Turk.
- [The PLY Polygon File Format](https://gamma-web.iacs.umd.edu/POWERPLANT/papers/ply.pdf)
- Supports C3/C10 inferences: vertices/faces and optional properties describe geometry; core PLY supplies neither mandatory physical units nor scene transformation matrices. Custom properties are possible. The extension alone does not establish clinical semantics, registration provenance, internal anatomy, force, or motion.

### S19 — Geometry reporting boundary (synthesis)

- **Type:** engineering inference from S15–S18 and the repository's stated geometry limits; not an additional clinical study.
- Registered arches can support **sampled surface proximity in the recorded pose**. A buccal bite surface can support registration and may duplicate arch anatomy; do not treat it as a third opposing jaw. Sampling does not establish exact contact area or overlap volume. Apparent penetration may arise from scan/registration error; absent sampled collision does not prove functional clearance. Report **file units** until physical units are independently established. Keep the original pose distinguishable from offsets.

### S20 — Manufacturer: scanbody and connection level

- **Date:** document 702063/en/D/03, September 2023. **Type:** Straumann technical instructions; system-specific.
- [Intraoral scanbody instructions](https://www.straumann.com/content/dam/media-center/straumann/en/documents/brochure/technical-information/702063-en_low.pdf)
- Supports C4/C11: scanbodies encode implant/analog/abutment position and orientation, with separate implant- and abutment-level components and software compatibility requirements. The visible crown/gum scan alone does not establish the hidden connection. Verify the exact selected hardware's current instructions rather than generalizing this manual's historical component details.

### S21 — Manufacturer: current library entry point

- **Date:** publication/update date not displayed; accessed 2026-10-24. **Type:** Straumann product documentation.
- [Metal Scanbody and CADCAM libraries](https://www.straumann.com/us/en/dental-professionals/digital-performance/consumables/scanbodies-analogs-sleeves/metal-scanbody.html)
- Supports C11: directs users to current libraries for compatible scanbody/CAD workflows. Product availability and versions can change; record the actual library used. This is not independent clinical accuracy evidence.

### S22 — Manufacturer: restorative manufacturing constraints

- **Date:** February 2019, version 1, NAMLIT/NAMIT.1084. **Type:** historical Straumann Variobase technical information.
- [Variobase Basic Information](https://www.straumann.com/content/dam/media-center/straumann/en-us/documents/brochure/product-information/NAMLIT-1084-Variobase-Basic-Information.pdf)
- Supports C7/C11: illustrates component-specific implant kits, scanbody matching, screw-channel geometry, processing, and material-manufacturer minimum wall thickness. Supports the need for real system/material constraints; do not reuse its numerical machining, screw, or clearance settings as universal or assume this version remains current.

## Reusable wording boundaries

- Say **“consideration for clinician review”**, **“reported association”**, **“consensus recommendation for [population/protocol]”**, and **“sampled mesh proximity.”** Preserve population, outcome, and evidence type when using a numerical result.
- Do not claim **“safe fixture size/depth from PLY,” “exact contact area,” “bite force,” “clinically validated occlusion,”** or **“manufacturable implant restoration”** from generic mesh operations.
- When only upper/lower/bite PLY files exist, surface inspection and questions about missing clinical records are supportable. Definitive surgical sizing/placement, tissue diagnosis, and dynamic occlusal clearance remain unresolved by those inputs.
